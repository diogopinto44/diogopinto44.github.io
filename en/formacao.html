Choosing Digital Marketing

Continuing my studies beyond my bachelor’s degree had always been a goal of mine. I chose the master’s in Digital Marketing because it brought together my interest in marketing and my aptitude for digital tools and technology. When I considered the area in which I wanted to deepen my education, this specialisation felt right for the path I wanted to follow.

Balancing work and a master’s degree

I started my internship at Grupo Ramos Ferreira before beginning the master’s degree. I already knew I wanted to apply and felt supported in doing so from the outset. Starting the course brought a mixture of pride and happiness, but also anxiety about organising my time and balancing both responsibilities.

It was a demanding and exhausting period. There were nights with little rest to meet university deadlines and longer working days to fulfil my commitments to the company. Balancing these demands required me to organise my time, meet objectives and complete tasks on schedule, often under pressure.

At the same time, studying while working created a direct connection between education and practice. I applied what I was learning in the master’s at the company and brought practical market experience into my academic assignments. That exchange helped me develop in both settings and gave meaning to the effort this period required. Despite the strain, the challenge of meeting both sets of responsibilities strengthened my determination to keep going.

At Grupo Ramos Ferreira, I applied SEO strategies and improved the content strategy based on what I was learning in the master’s programme. I also developed my ability to analyse digital metrics.

Continuing to learn

In the future, I am considering complementing my academic education with postgraduate programmes, courses and other training that could add value to my career. Graphic design and photography are two areas in which I would like to develop my knowledge.

Featured research · Master’s dissertation

Digital Strategies for Building Brand Awareness: The Case of Construction and Engineering.

ISCAP · October 2025
Supervisor: Professor Jorge Remondes
Dissertation grade: 18/20

Writing and defending my dissertation ↗

I examined how four digital strategies relate to brand awareness and corporate image in the construction and engineering sector: SEO, content marketing, omnichannel integration and AI-powered personalisation.

Why I chose this topic

I chose this topic because of my work at Grupo Ramos Ferreira and the difficulties I encountered in building brand awareness in this sector. I wanted to understand that challenge better and explore practical solutions relevant to my professional context. My familiarity with the sector and access to information and professional contacts helped me carry out the study.

How I conducted the research

I used a mixed-methods approach, combining semi-structured interviews with professionals from the sector and other B2B markets with an online questionnaire for construction and engineering companies. The questionnaire was administered between April and July 2025, after contacting more than 500 companies.

Of the 100 responses received, 57 met the inclusion criteria. I excluded 35 responses from companies without a marketing department or designated marketing professional, and a further 8 from companies that did not use digital marketing strategies.

The analysis combined thematic interpretation of the interviews with descriptive statistics and partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) in JASP. This approach brought professionals’ perspectives together with the patterns identified in the questionnaire.

Key findings

  • Channel integration and SEO

    In the estimated model, integration across digital and traditional channels showed the strongest relationship with brand awareness, followed by SEO strategies.

  • Brand awareness and corporate image

    Brand awareness was positively associated with corporate image, highlighting the importance of building recognition among a company’s audiences.

  • Content marketing and AI-powered personalisation

    In this sample, the direct effects of these two strategies on brand awareness were not statistically significant in the model. In the interviews, however, project-based content was valued for its contribution to credibility and trust.

  • The role of marketing in the sector

    The interviews highlighted brand recognition, credibility within the sector and employer branding as relevant dimensions for assessing communication in a context where professional relationships and procurement processes play an important role.

Practical implications

The findings suggest priorities that can inform marketing strategy in the sector:

  • Coordinate digital and traditional channels to maintain consistent communication throughout interactions with clients and partners.
  • Develop SEO and content that demonstrates the company’s technical expertise, projects and capabilities.
  • Assess communication with attention to brand awareness, credibility and positioning.
Study limitations and future research

The analysis used a convenience sample of companies in Portugal that already had a marketing department or designated marketing professional and used digital strategies. It included both exclusively B2B organisations and companies serving B2B and B2C markets. The study did not track effects over time, and its findings should be interpreted in the context of this sample.

The dissertation proposes extending the research to other markets, tracking the evolution of strategies over time and exploring the role of resources, team training and digital maturity.

Writing and defending my dissertation

Working in the sector helped me find the question I wanted to investigate. Finding time to put the answer into words was harder. Balancing the dissertation with my job and my personal life was exhausting. I took an extension so I could go back over the text carefully and submit the best work I could.

At the defence, I realised how well I knew my own work. I felt confident explaining the choices I had made, from my first question to how I interpreted the results. After so many hours of writing, I was proud to show the work behind those pages. I earned 18/20 for the dissertation and came away from the defence with more confidence in myself.

DEMS

Developing E-marketing Skills for the Business Market

During my master’s degree, I was part of Market Masters, a team of five ISCAP students. As part of DEMS, an Erasmus+ project focused on digital marketing for small and medium-sized businesses, we prepared a proposal for Advanced Cyclone Systems (ACS). The company develops particle separation systems for industry.

My team’s placement

From the first click to an enquiry

Our challenge was to help people searching for a cyclone system find ACS, understand whether its technology suited their application and make an enquiry. We reviewed the website and LinkedIn’s public ad library. One ad took readers to the annual newsletter but gave them no clear way to subscribe to the next issue. We proposed a sign-up page with a short form so readers would have a next step and ACS could measure how many subscriptions the campaign generated.

A plan for the first six months

For the first three months, we proposed a website audit, an editorial calendar and campaigns on LinkedIn and Google. Over the following three months, the ACS team could use the data to refine the campaigns. Increasing organic traffic and attracting qualified leads were goals of the plan; the report does not present implementation results.

Explore the team’s recommendations
  • Organic search and website performance

    Our Lighthouse audit showed that the website could load faster on mobile. We recommended reducing image sizes and rewriting the homepage headings and copy to better match searches such as “cyclone system”.

  • Social media and content

    We compared how ACS and three competitors communicated online. One idea we proposed was “Feedback Talks”: a monthly feature in which a customer would share their experience, with excerpts from their account presented as a social media carousel.

  • Email marketing

    We proposed grouping contacts by interest and testing two subject lines for the same email. Open and click rates would help the team choose subject lines and content for future emails.

  • Automation and artificial intelligence

    We explored how a virtual assistant could answer common questions about the separation systems and identify visitors who needed to speak to the ACS team.

BeeSimple

From Portuguese honey to a product concept

BeeSimple grew out of a challenge connected to Portuguese beekeeping: finding a new way to promote Portuguese honey. Early in the degree, our group developed a natural honey-flavoured ice lolly, connecting the product idea with our first foundations in strategy and branding.

What we developed

  • Analysis of the context, target audience and competitors, including a SWOT analysis.
  • Product concept and identity development: name, logo, slogan and packaging.
  • Pricing, distribution and communication proposals, bringing together the different marketing-mix decisions.

My contribution

As I recall, I took part in several stages of the group project, with greater involvement in research, strategy and presenting the project.

What I took from this experience

It was one of my first opportunities to work through several marketing stages within a single project. It helped me connect strategy with product development and the way a product is presented to its audience.

02 · Year 2 · Semester 1 · Services Marketing

Agência Abreu

Communicating services through email marketing

With stronger foundations in place, we developed a communication strategy for Agência Abreu, selected as the service business for our project. The aim was to explore customer retention, acquisition and closer relationships through emails and text messages created in E-goi.

What we developed

  • Analysis of the company and its service, using segmentation, positioning, SWOT and PESTE analysis.
  • Creation of a form and contact segmentation by destination preference: tropical, city, rural and snow holidays.
  • Development of personalised emails, seasonal campaigns and SMS proposals, reviewing the statistics available in the platform.

The practical work took place in an academic setting, using a contact list of classmates. The report documents those tests; the data shown does not come from Agência Abreu’s customer database.

My contribution

My contribution focused on creating the emails and managing the E-goi platform used for the project.

What I took from this experience

This project taught me how to work with email marketing tools and deepened my understanding of services marketing strategy. Contact with a real company made what we were learning more concrete.

03 · Year 3 · Brand Management

KAMALY

Creating a brand, connecting strategy and design

In my final year, KAMALY brought together much of what I had learned through a project to create a yoga clothing brand. It was perhaps the most complex assignment of the degree, connecting strategy, concept development and design execution.

We collaborated with students from the integrated master’s in Textile Engineering at the University of Minho, who were responsible for the technical product development. Our group at ISCAP worked on creating and developing the brand, connecting the clothing’s characteristics to its identity and value proposition.

What we developed

  • Definition of the brand concept, values, audiences and positioning.
  • Creation of the name, logo and tagline, including visual development and applications on the clothing.
  • Coordination of product, pricing, distribution and communication, including digital materials and proposals for brand development.

My contribution

I contributed across all areas handled by our group at ISCAP: brand strategy, naming and visual identity, garment design and communication materials.

What I took from this experience

KAMALY allowed me to bring together knowledge from several subjects and work with another field of study. It was an opportunity to develop strategic and creative skills and understand how a brand concept translates into design decisions.
